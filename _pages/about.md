Ajax Benander is a graduate student in the Department of Physics at Brown University, where he entered the Physics Sc.M. program in September 2026. He completed a B.S. in Applied Mathematics at the Rochester Institute of Technology in May 2026, with a minor in Philosophy and an immersion in Physics.

His research takes tools from mathematical physics, modal logic, probability, and philosophy of science to the foundations of physics. The central framework, **Aethic Reasoning**, treats an observer-indexed information state as a mathematical object and asks what follows from the consistency constraints such states must satisfy. Current manuscripts develop this idea into a counterfactual semantics based on perturbing information states rather than ranking possible worlds; an algebra of informational refinement, composition, and weighting; and a proposed re-axiomatization of quantum measurement on a finite path-kernel domain. In the quantum work, ideal projective conditioning is represented as pruning followed by renormalization and record structures induce self-adjoint observables; completeness of the state and observable representations, and the bridge from formal invalidity to physical unrealizability, remain open questions.

A second line of work, [**Nexic Reasoning**](https://philpapers.org/rec/BENNRD), develops Bayesian typicality over partial information states and applies it to natural history, astrobiology, and the Fermi paradox. Its central Accordance Principle constrains how prior-improbable realized attributes must relate to an observer- or target-conditioned sample. More recent work extends the same logic to future-targeted and survival-conditioned processes, while separating general probabilistic theorems from empirical hypotheses about Earth history.

The origin of the research program traces back to pre-college work on anthropic reasoning and natural history. The earliest surviving Nexic materials date to 2020-2021. In August 2022, during Ajax's first week at RIT, work on the double-slit experiment led to the state-relative treatment of superposition that became Aethic Reasoning. By May 2023 the framework had been organized around three postulates, including a reflexive-transitive refinement structure used in the pruning rule. A supervised study with [Dr. Ernest Fokoué](https://www.rit.edu/directory/epfeqa-ernest-fokoue) at RIT in 2024 helped translate the pre-existing structural picture into conventional mathematical prose and publication form.

The research program is independent and, except where otherwise noted, has not yet undergone peer review. Current work focuses on sharpening theorem scope, separating mathematical results from conjectural or empirical extensions, and developing the remaining quantitative and physical questions. The current Aethic paper set is collected on the [short-papers page](https://aethus.org/short-papers/), with the comprehensive treatment and overview at the top and the self-contained papers below. The long-form [Nexic Reasoning](https://philpapers.org/rec/BENNRD) preprint remains the source-of-record treatment for the older anthropic wing. Historical and superseded versions remain available through [PhilPapers](https://philpeople.org/profiles/ajax-benander/publications) and the public research archive.
