Ajax Benander is a graduate student in physics at Brown University (M.Sc., entering September 2026). He completed a B.S. in Mathematics at the Rochester Institute of Technology in 2026, with a minor in Philosophy.

His research takes the tools of modal logic — possibility, counterfactuals, what an agent has records of — to the foundations of physics, treating an observer's information state as a mathematical object and asking what follows from the consistency constraints such states must satisfy. The first framework, Aethic Reasoning, develops three such constraints into a counterfactual semantics with no possible worlds and no similarity ordering, and, with the path-integral kernel added, into an axiomatization of quantum mechanics in which the Dirac–von Neumann formalism is recovered axiom by axiom with the projection postulate, the measurement axiom, and the observables axiom as theorems. The second, [Nexic Reasoning](https://philpapers.org/rec/BENNRD), relocates anthropic typicality from physical constants to circumstantial historical events, yielding a calculus over natural history with applications to paleontology and the Fermi paradox. The main paper is [*Aethic Reasoning: A Comprehensive Solution to the Quantum Measurement Problem* (PhilArchive, first deposited 2024; current version 2026)](https://philpapers.org/rec/BENARA-5); the work is independent and not yet peer-reviewed.

The origin of the research program traces back to 2018, when Ajax, then a high school student in Massachusetts, came across the [Doomsday argument online](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qXXZLoq2zFc&t=175s) and began extending it to a paleontological question: could the existence and extinction of the dinosaurs be understood as a prerequisite to human existence? That question and its answer were originally one and the same idea, only later disentangled into a general principle (the Accordance Principle) and a specific application (the dinosaurian two-node derivation) throughout 2021. The broader framework built around it became Nexic Reasoning, with the earliest written record dating to June 2020. In August 2022, during his first week at RIT, a chance encounter with a [Jim Al-Khalili online lecture on the double-slit experiment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A9tKncAdlHQ) catalyzed the Aethic Extrusion Principle: the idea that gaining information doesn't collapse a wavefunction in one's current universe, but shifts the observer to a different block universe whose entire history is already consistent with the new knowledge. Ten days later, on August 22, 2022, a thought experiment about running a double-slit experiment with human subjects on the soccer field outside his dorm room led to the first recognition of the need for a Third Postulate. The completed derivation of Aethic Reasoning came together on May 8, 2023.

Ajax has a lifelong relationship with mathematical structure that he describes as fundamentally synesthetic. By high school, he had independently arrived at constructions he would later learn were called equivalence classes, Markov chains, and commutative rings, all without formal training in abstract algebra. His advisor [Dr. Ernest Fokoué](https://www.rit.edu/directory/epfeqa-ernest-fokoue) at RIT was instrumental in helping translate these internal structural intuitions into communicable prose during a supervised study in 2024. He has since posted preprints across PhilArchive and PhilSci-Archive, presented at the RIT Math Club, and built an interactive webpage for exploring the framework's classification theorem on multi-slit experiments. The framework's development is documented in a dated, checkable record: the pruning rule's mechanism in a journal entry of August 22, 2022; the three postulates complete on May 8, 2023; the formal apparatus built afterward on that unchanged base.

He is currently working on Active Reasoning, the program that takes up the one thing the axiomatization posits: the kernel, whose derivation is assigned to a geometric arena that the postulates' interacting regime toggles visibility into, on the argument that the postulates are logical in substance where the kernel is kinematic. Outside of research, Ajax is interested in video production, science communication, and spending unreasonable amounts of time staring at Mach-Zehnder interferometer animations.
